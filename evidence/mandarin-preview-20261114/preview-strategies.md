# Recorded Qwen preview replay

Eighteen native original-rate traces from build 442 (15 panel samples plus three extended samples) were replayed at their recorded worker-event timestamps through the actual Swift preview gate. This isolates publication policy from inference, microphone operation and UI drawing. The JSON binds every input to the trace hash, wave hash and original app revision. A nineteenth trace independently reproduces the 3.09 s stall on a repeat run of the same 38.8 s WAV; it is excluded from cohort aggregates.

The candidate permits a Chinese comma, period, enumeration comma or letter-delimited Chinese double dash at the same already-observed position when the protected words match. Every accepted intermediate string is a literal prefix of that pass. It withholds only the provisional terminal Chinese period after first display, retains internal punctuation and delivers the entire final result. Stable dispatch length on a Qwen partial stays zero.

- The largest reproducible gap in the repeat run was 26.521–29.610 s. The candidate can also publish the real tails at 27.099, 28.278 and 28.954 s. One intervening hypothesis rewrites an old word and is still withheld.
- Across the original 18 traces, pooled intervals between previously unseen useful bigrams have P95 1.401 s for the original and 1.080 s for the candidate. A useful event is defined in the repository cadence scorer; the untranscribed trailing-quiet diagnostic uses novel lexical bigrams without a reference. For the 17 transcribed clips, all 670 shared reference-bigram occurrences remain visible before the end and 11 appear at least 250 ms earlier; none appear that much later. First previews and all final hypothesis events are identical on every trace.
- Among consecutive partials, the previous text ending in a Chinese period and then extending at exactly that punctuation site (with the period removed) occurs 198 versus 13 times. Literal non-prefix replacements, including punctuation, are 292 versus 120. The separate punctuation-normalized pre-end lexical replacement count is 52 versus 53. The larger improvement concerns visual punctuation churn and time to new words, not a measured decrease in lexical correction frequency.
- Withholding one eligible final Han character on every pass reduced lexical replacements to 34, but delayed 163 reference-bigram occurrences and omitted 14 during speech. Withholding on an already-rewritten tail still delayed 47. Restricting this to a single character after a phrase boundary delayed one reference occurrence without improving coverage or the worst pause. A one-pass hold on non-growing local corrections also delayed five. None is enabled.
- Reference-prefix distance at 10 Hz, taken only on the 17 labeled clips, is diagnostic and is not archival CER. Before/after rates are 0.121835 and 0.121765 errors per displayed character. Accuracy, acoustic energy and device evidence for a new build require separate native execution.

The production gate compiled from ac927aa and the selected scoring variant produced exactly equal event sequences on all 19 streams. The direct Swift runner executed three existing ownership regressions and three new cases for punctuation release, protected numeric input and exact first/final observations.
