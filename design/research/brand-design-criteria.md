# Saytile — evidence-led identity criteria

This standard is a working gate for design exploration, not a claim that any current candidate is approved.

## 1. Identity hierarchy

1. Distinctive silhouette and optical balance.
2. Memorability and ownability.
3. Proportion, negative space and craft.
4. Appropriate emotional tone for a calm, always-available consumer product.
5. Product association through the broader system—not literal feature illustration.

A logo that explains speech but is not beautiful fails. A beautiful generic glyph with no coherent relationship to Saytile also fails.

## 2. Hard exclusions

Do not reuse or disguise the previously rejected directions: Folding Flow, Living Line, Speech-to-Text pictograms, ST ligature with coral dot, Live Surface, ordinary S-ribbon, folded paper, stacked cards, microphone, waveform, generic AI star, purple glass orb/panel.

Also reject common category shorthand: equalizer bars, chat bubbles, text-line containers, generic app windows, play/record buttons, generic infinity loops, generic rounded-square mazes, decorative dots and status halos.

## 3. Form tests before color

- Solid black on white, and white on black.
- Silhouette recognizable without wordmark.
- Distinct at 16, 20, 24, 32, 48 and 64 px.
- No accidental star, speech bubble, microphone, letter S, generic loop, or generic abstract-AI resemblance.
- Negative space should be intentional and stable at small sizes.
- Geometric construction should be explainable by a small number of coherent curves or shapes, not arbitrary ornament.
- Check optical balance rather than relying only on mathematically centered geometry.

## 4. Wordmark tests

- Use a custom or materially modified wordmark; no unmodified default lowercase font.
- Evaluate Saytile and saytile separately.
- Tune letter spacing, x-height, a, y, t, and terminal shapes.
- Check pairing with the independent mark and standalone wordmark.
- No unnecessary tagline or decorative dot.

## 5. Product UI tests

- Main surface remains transcript-first.
- No logo repeated in the transcript area.
- Brand color is used sparingly: active state, one restrained accent, selected control, or focus detail.
- Text contrast and reading comfort take precedence over brand saturation.
- Light and dark appearances must both remain calm.
- The brand mark may appear in the menu bar and onboarding, but it must not become another persistent control.

## 6. Color decision framework

Compare at least five clearly distinct families on the same mark and the same panel:
- warm mineral / vermilion family
- cool ultramarine / cobalt family
- deep teal / jade family
- warm chartreuse / olive family
- neutral ink / graphite with a single accent

Do not assume one family is the answer. Compare brand recall, emotional temperature, text contrast, dark-mode behavior, Dock recognition and long-session comfort. Avoid saturated backgrounds behind continuous transcript text.

## 7. Evaluation rubric

Score each candidate 1–5 on:
- first-glance beauty
- silhouette distinctiveness
- geometric resolution
- negative-space quality
- small-size survival
- category differentiation
- wordmark fit
- product UI fit
- light/dark adaptability

Automatic rejection if any of: black/white form <4, 16px survival <3, or category differentiation <3. Scores are a design audit, not a substitute for human taste. Do not advance a candidate merely because its explanation is compelling.

## 8. Source-grounded design lessons

- Separate brand mark from transient interaction: Wispr Flow.
- Sculptural silhouette can be more ownable than a literal icon: Superwhisper, but avoid its loop.
- Narrow persistent surfaces need spatial restraint and low-friction focus behavior: SideNotes.
- Contextual controls should remain secondary: PopClip.
- Typographic rhythm and content legibility create trust: MacWhisper, Paste, Raycast.
- Clear space, disciplined monochrome use and consistency build confidence: Linear.
- Warmth comes from spacing, hierarchy and purposeful accent use, not generic gradients: Craft, Things 3, Granola.

## 9. Approval rule

No candidate is a brand asset until it passes the black/white, 16px, wordmark, app-icon and real-panel checks. No palette is approved until the same design is compared in at least five color families. The production app and main branch remain untouched until explicit user approval.
